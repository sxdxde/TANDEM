6 · M6: per-layer entries, sparse reads, measured INT4 error

M5 left three open questions. Does averaging over layers hide a placement win? Does the dynamic policy still matter if a system reads only the entries that matter? And how wrong is the assumed INT4 error? M6 answers each. The protocol is M5's, fixed in advance: thresholds in multiples of uniform attention, chosen on chart and scene as the lowest energy with mean quality no worse than static, then tested on the held-out noise_label trace. The run is 408 simulations (results/m6_sweep.csv).

What we built

  • Sparse access. Step 0 is dense. After that only the top 10% of active entries by live importance EMA, plus entries created in the last step, are read. Skipped attention mass is reported and costs no energy. It does not depend on placement (tested).
  • Assumption: the importance estimate sees the true attention weights at zero cost. That is an upper bound on what a real approximate scorer could do.
  • Per-layer entries. One entry per token and layer. Each layer's attention is renormalised and divided by the number of layers, so a step's mass still sums to 1. Entry size matches a single-entry token, so absolute energies are about 30× the layer-averaged ones. Compare ratios only.
  • Quality is linear in attention weights, so static policies score identically in both views (0.0031 on chart). Per-layer entries change only what the dynamic policy can do.

Strict rule, marginal miss. On the held-out trace the per-layer dense configuration saves 20.0% energy and loses 0.8% on quality against static. The rule fixed in advance was quality no worse than static, so this misses by 0.8%. We report it as a miss and do not round it to zero. Quality was −2.0% and −10.3% on the two training traces.

Mechanism: arithmetic inference, not instrumented. In the static per-layer baseline the roughly 1.3k HOT text entries (44 tokens × 30 layers) cost about 170M of about 380M pJ. The dynamic policy saves about 78M pJ, roughly half of that. The WARM share of attention barely moves (6.2% to 6.1%). From these totals, the saving comes mostly from demoting text entries in layers where text is unimportant, plus a few promotions of visual entries in layers that attend to them. It does not come mainly from rescuing salient visual tokens. This is the same text-demotion channel seen on the synthetic trace. We did not break the saving down per entry to confirm it.

Measured INT4 error

We quantized SmolVLM's prefill K and V per token to INT4 (min-max) and measured relative L2 error, over 30 layers and 3 images (results/int4_error.csv, 360 rows). At group size 32 the error is 0.102 for K and 0.081 for V. At group size 64 it is 0.134 and 0.094. Visual and text tokens agree within about 1%.

The assumed 0.05 was about half the measured value of roughly 0.09. We left the config at 0.05. Quality loss is the sum of attention weight times error, so it is linear in the error and the same factor applies to every policy. Every ratio against static is unchanged, and so are the threshold choices, since the selection rule compares ratios. Only absolute quality values scale, by about 1.8×. Read any absolute quality number in this report that way. Keeping 0.05 means the committed results still reproduce.

Two limits. We did not compute a per-channel variant for K, because a guard skipped it for token counts that are not a multiple of 64. And relative L2 error stands in for quality loss here, the same proxy the rest of the report uses.

8 · Limits and next steps

Limits

  • The real-trace evidence is thin: one 256M model, three generated images, 64 decode steps, selection on two traces and one held-out trace. Natural photos, documents and larger models are untested.
  • The synthetic M4 conclusions describe the synthetic trace. They should not be read as properties of vision-language models.
  • The config still carries the assumed 0.05 INT4 error. Measured error is about 0.09, so absolute quality values are about 1.8× too small. Ratios against static are unaffected. The per-channel K variant was not measured.
  • Latency is serialized total work, not wall-clock time.
  • Per-layer entries average over heads within a layer. Per-head structure is still hidden.
  • Sparse reads assume a perfect importance estimate at zero cost, which is an upper bound for any real scorer. The 50%, 20% and 5% top-k numbers come from an unsaved ad-hoc run.
  • The per-layer dense gain needs about a thousand tier moves per session, and it narrowly misses the strict quality rule.
  • The mechanism behind the per-layer gain is inferred from totals, not instrumented.
  • No quality-aware policy exists yet. The policy never sees quality.
  • The planned oracle comparison against ground-truth top-k attended tokens is not done.

Next steps

  • A layer-aware static prior (modality × layer) as a stronger baseline than static modality. The per-layer dynamic win looks like a layer effect, so this baseline may capture it without any moves.
  • Per-head entries.
  • A real approximate importance estimator for sparse reads, in place of true weights.
  • Sweep move cost and top-k fraction on real traces.
  • Natural images, documents and a larger vision-language model.
  • The oracle top-k comparison from the M5 plan, and a quality-aware promotion rule.

9 · Conclusion

A fixed split by modality does most of the work. On real traces it cuts energy by about 90% against keeping everything HOT, for a quality loss near 0.003, and sparse reads shrink the remaining gap between flat and static from about 9.3× to under 2×.

The dynamic policy's synthetic trade-off (about −49% quality loss for about +55% energy) did not replicate. The synthetic trace sent about 68% of attention to WARM, and real traces send about 6%. On real traces its value is conditional. With per-layer entries and dense reads it saves about 20% energy at roughly neutral quality, narrowly missing the strict rule, and likely by demoting text entries rather than rescuing visual ones. With sparse reads that benefit vanishes, and it needs about a thousand moves per session.

The INT4 error we assumed was about half the measured value. That does not change any ratio. The next experiment worth running is a layer-aware static prior, since it would show whether the dynamic policy's remaining gain needs dynamics at all.
